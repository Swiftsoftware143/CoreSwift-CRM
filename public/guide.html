API Overview

All endpoints sit under /api/:

  • Public: auth/login, auth/register, auth/forgot-password, auth/reset-password
  • Protected: All others require Authorization: Bearer <token>

1b MCP — connect your AI tools

Your workspace has an API key you can hand to an MCP client — Claude Code, Cursor, Hermes, or your own agent — so it can read your CoreSwift data.

  • Endpoint: https://hooks.swiftsoftware.net/mcp
  • Token: your API key. Open MCP in the console, press View & copy, and re-enter your password to see the full key.
  • Read-only: every tool is scoped to your workspace and can only read. There is no write tool and no free-form SQL door.

1c Data mapping — bring another app’s data in

Your other SwiftSoftware apps can send their leads into this CRM. Data mapping is where you decide which of their fields lands in which of your contact fields — no code, and nothing to redo when they add a new question.

To build one:

  1. Settings → Data mapping. Pick the app and the kind of row you want (its leads, its clients, its contacts).
  2. The left column lists the fields that app sends. If it has already sent you data, the list is built from what actually arrived. A field it has never sent cannot be listed yet — it appears by itself after the first push that carries it.
  3. Click a source field to drop it into a destination as a pill, or type plain text around it. A pill always deletes whole, so a token can never be cut in half.
  4. The right column shows exactly what CoreSwift would receive, and updates as you go. Nothing is written to a contact from this screen.
  5. Choose what happens on a match — the same person already in your CRM: Update (fill in what arrived), Skip (leave them alone) or Create new.
  6. Save, then press Test with sample row to see the server agree with the preview.

Things worth knowing:

  • Extra data goes to metadata.<name>, so storing a new field never needs a database change.
  • A required field that arrives empty refuses the whole push and names the field — you never get a half-written contact.
  • “Create new” cannot make a second contact with the same email. One contact per email is this account’s rule, so it says so plainly instead of quietly updating the existing one.
  • Runs lists the last 50 attempts with what was refused and why. A mapping that has never succeeded says so rather than looking healthy.
  • Data only flows in. Sister apps push to this CRM; nothing here sends contacts back out.

🗂️ Workspaces & switching

One account, several workspaces

Your account is the business. A workspace lives inside it and holds the work — contacts, companies, mail, calendars, pipelines and automations. Settings, your plan, branding and team members are shared across every workspace in the account. Every account starts with one workspace called My Workspace, so you never land on a blank screen.

The 🗂️ menu in the top bar, next to your plan, is where workspaces are managed:

  • Switch — move between workspaces. The console reloads to show the chosen workspace's work, and your settings stay the same.
  • Add workspace — create another. How many your plan allows decides the ceiling.
  • Rename — change a workspace's name (owner or admin).
  • Make default — mark which workspace a fresh sign-in opens. Exactly one is default at a time.
  • Delete — remove a workspace you no longer need (owner or admin).

A few rules are always enforced: an account always keeps at least one workspace; you cannot delete the workspace you are currently signed into (switch to another first); and two workspaces in one account cannot share a name. Team members can switch and add workspaces; only an owner or admin can rename, delete or change the default.

Finding Duplicate Contacts

Over time the same person can end up in your workspace more than once — imported from a spreadsheet on Monday, added by hand on Friday. The Duplicates screen (in the sidebar, directly under Contacts) shows you every set of records that look like the same person so you can decide what to keep.

It compares two things:

  • The same phone number, once punctuation and spacing are ignored — (561) 555-0143 and 5615550143 count as the same number.
  • The same first and last name, once case and extra spaces are ignored.

Email addresses are deliberately not compared: a workspace can never hold two active contacts with the same email address, so a report on email could only ever come back empty.

Nothing is merged automatically. Each row has an Edit button that opens that contact in the normal editor, so you decide which record survives and what it says. If a group names a record you cannot see in the list above it, the record is still there — the name lookup stops after 500 contacts, and the screen says so rather than pretending the contact is missing.

If you see "Account matching is not available on your current plan", the report is switched on for paid plans only. The screen tells you that instead of showing an empty list, because "you have no duplicates" and "you are not allowed to look" are different answers.

🆘 Customer Support Portal

Your Help Desk — Built In

CoreSwift CRM includes a complete customer support ticketing system. Replace Freshdesk, Zendesk, or your overloaded email inbox. Your customers submit requests. Your team manages them. Nothing leaves the platform.

No API keys. No chatbot plugins. No external subscriptions. It just works.

How Tickets Come In

Tickets can arrive through multiple channels — all tracked by source so you know where every request came from:

ChannelSource CodeHow
👤 ManualmanualYour team creates tickets in the admin Support panel
🌐 Contact FormformDrop our embed widget on any website — visitors submit tickets without logging in. Anyone who writes from the contact page is handed a reference, and can come back to that page with the reference and their email address to read your team's answer — no sign-in needed.
📧 EmailemailForward your support@ inbox to our parser — emails become tickets automatically
🤖 ChatbotagentConnect any chatbot or AI agent via our public API — tickets created programmatically

None of these channels is privileged. A ticket created by your chatbot, your contact form, or your staff member is treated identically. No special routing, no hidden costs.

Embed Widget — One Line on Your Website

Add a floating support button to any website with one script tag:

<script src="https://coreswiftcrm.com/s/YOUR_TENANT_ID/widget.js"></script>

That gives you:

  • Floating button — bottom-right corner, matches your brand
  • Slide-out form — name, email, subject, description, priority
  • Success confirmation — visitor knows their request was received
  • No dependencies — vanilla JS, no framework, no CORS issues
  • No login required — your visitors submit tickets without accounts

Admin Support Panel

Your team manages everything from 🎫 Support in the sidebar:

  • Full queue — all tickets, filterable by status and priority
  • Create tickets — manually open tickets during phone calls
  • Reply inline — threaded messages on every ticket
  • Status workflow — open → in progress → resolved → closed
  • Priority levels — low, medium, high, urgent
  • Assignment — route tickets to team members

Customer Portal

Your end customers open 💬 My Support and see only their own requests. The portal lives at /s/<your-tenant-id>/support, and your team can open it from Support Tickets → 💬 My Support.

There is no password to issue and no email needed to sign in: the customer enters their email address plus the reference of a request they already have (the first 8 characters of the ticket id, shown on the ticket in your console). That pair proves the address, and the server hands back an access grant that never travels in a URL.

  • Submit new requests — with priority selection
  • View their history — all past tickets, statuses visible
  • Reply to open tickets — threaded conversation with your team; replying to a resolved request reopens it
  • Tenant-scoped — customers only see their own data, never another customer's and never another tenant's

This is their personal help desk — not your admin panel. They manage their own support experience.

API for Integrations

For programmatic access — connecting chatbots, Zapier, custom apps:

POST /s/:tenant_id/ticket
GET  /s/:tenant_id/widget.js
POST /api/public/contact

Set source: "agent" when creating tickets from chatbots or AI systems. The public endpoints require no authentication — designed for third-party consumption.

Pricing

Free tier: The help desk is not included — the ticket queue and the admin support panel refuse a Free workspace with 402 Payment Required (GET /api/tickets: "Support tickets is not available on your current plan"), so Support Tickets → 💬 My Support is unavailable too.
Paid tier: Support tickets (embed widget + admin support panel + customer portal) + custom domain (use your own support.yourdomain.com) + email-to-ticket parsing + priority support.

The public intake arms are not plan-gated, by decision: the embed widget script at /s/<tenant-id>/widget.js and the customer portal page are served on every plan, and a visitor's request is accepted and stored — a customer's request is never refused because of your plan, so nothing is lost either way. What a Free workspace gets instead of silence: the Dashboard shows a Customer requests you cannot open notice naming how many requests are waiting, the workspace dropdown flags them (⚠ cannot open), and GET /api/auth/me/usage carries the three fields the notice is built from (tickets, tickets_readable, support_intake_unreadable). Stored requests appear in Support Tickets the moment the plan includes it.

Chatbot integrations are API-only on all tiers — you connect your own chatbot, we handle the ticket routing.

13 Webhooks

Webhook Overview

CoreSwift CRM supports both incoming and outgoing webhooks for integration with satellite apps and external systems.

Satellite Apps: FunnelSwift, IncentiveSwift, WorkflowSwift, and MissedCall Respondr can push data into CoreSwift via inbound webhooks.

Inbound Webhooks (Receiving Data)

Other apps can send events to CoreSwift using authenticated webhook endpoints. The URL carries only the key prefix, which is a public index; the credential is the full key in the X-Satellite-Key header, and its SHA-256 digest must equal the digest stored for that prefix. A missing header or a wrong key is refused with 401.

POST /inbound/:key_prefix/:event_type
X-Satellite-Key: <full-api-key>
Content-Type: application/json

{ "payload": "data" }

Or the v2 endpoint:

POST /inbound/v2/:key_prefix/:event_type
X-Satellite-Key: <full-api-key>

And the v3 contact-sync endpoint — POST /inbound/v3/:key_prefix/contact-sync (also survey-complete and contact-upsert) — with the same header.

These three routes are served by the CoreSwift application itself. They are not under /api/, and the public web vhosts proxy only /api/, /s/ and /track/ to it, so on the shared host a satellite app calls CoreSwift directly rather than through the public site.

Outgoing Webhook Endpoints (Sending Data)

You can register endpoints that CoreSwift will POST events to. CoreSwift delivers events to each endpoint that is active and subscribes to that event type. A workspace configures its own endpoints, signing secret and event subscriptions in the console under Settings → Webhooks: that screen lists each endpoint's name, URL, events, active state, last successful delivery and failure count, and can add, edit, pause, activate and delete them. Outbound webhooks are sold on Enterprise/Agency — a workspace whose plan lacks the webhooks module has its requests to the whole surface refused with HTTP 402 and the reason in the response body. The platform console (admin.coreswiftcrm.com) has no editor: the endpoints are workspace-scoped, so it only points here. These same calls remain available directly on the API:

POST /api/integrations/webhooks
Authorization: Bearer ***

{
  "name": "Slack Notifier",
  "url": "https://hooks.slack.com/services/xxx",
  "secret": "your-signing-secret",
  "events": ["contact.created", "opportunity.won"],
  "retry_count": 3,
  "timeout_ms": 5000
}

List configured webhooks:

GET /api/integrations/webhooks
Authorization: Bearer ***

Response: { "webhooks": [...] }

What arrives. Each delivery is a JSON POST:

POST <your url>
X-CoreSwift-Event: contact.created
X-CoreSwift-Delivery: <endpoint id>
X-CoreSwift-Delivery-Attempt: 1
X-CoreSwift-Signature: t=1759512345,v1=<hex>

{
  "event": "contact.created",
  "tenant_id": "...",
  "webhook_id": "...",
  "delivered_at": "2026-10-06T16:45:00+00:00",
  "data": { ...the event payload... }
}

Verifying the signature. When the endpoint has a signing secret the X-CoreSwift-Signature header is t=<unix seconds>,v1=<hex>, where v1 is HMAC-SHA256(secret, "<t>.<raw request body>") — compute the same value and reject a mismatch. An endpoint saved without a secret is sent unsigned.

Which events, and retries. An endpoint whose events list is empty receives every event; a non-empty list receives only those event types. An endpoint with is_active: false receives nothing. A failed delivery (anything other than a 2xx) is retried retry_count times with a growing backoff, each attempt bounded by timeout_ms; redirects are never followed. The endpoint's last_triggered_at shows its last successful delivery and failure_count how many deliveries have failed since the last success.

API Keys

Every account has one API key. That single key is the account's identity: the apps that work with CoreSwift (FunnelSwift, WorkflowSwift, IncentiveSwift, ADASwift, MissedCall Respondr) use it to send data in, and an AI client uses the same key for MCP. It has:

  • Prefix The leading characters, shown in the console. It is public and is not a secret.
  • Full Key The credential, sent as Authorization: Bearer <key>. The server stores only its SHA-256 digest (key_hash) and can never recover the key from the row; a sealed copy is kept so you can still view it.
  • Status Active, or gone once you revoke it (a revoked key authenticates nothing).
  • Last Used When it most recently authenticated something.

Exactly one per account. The database refuses a second active key. To replace it press Regenerate: the old key stops working the moment the new one exists, so update whatever was using it. The key is in Settings > Integration Center, and it is mirrored on the MCP page where View & copy shows it after you re-enter your password.

Which way the keys point. Your satellite apps use this key to send data into CoreSwift - CoreSwift does not mint a separate key for each of them. Going the other way, CoreSwift holds each satellite app's own key under its connection settings, so it can reach out to them.

Your inbox: bring your own domain

CoreSwift does not give out an address on our domain. You keep the domain you already own (for example yourbusiness.com) and your own Mailgun account, and your mail is delivered straight to you. Open the Shared Inbox tab in the console: the setup page is the onboarding, in three plain steps, with a copy button beside every value.

  1. Add your domain and make two choices. Type the domain, choose how it sends and how it receives, and paste only the keys those choices need. Both keys are stored sealed and used only for your own mail.
  2. Add the records at your own DNS host (Cloudflare, or your registrar). Three groups: MX — mxa.mailgun.org and mxb.mailgun.org, where mail is delivered; SPF and DKIM — v=spf1 include:mailgun.org ~all plus the DKIM value your own Mailgun mints, which prove the mail is really from you; and one CNAME — inbox → inbox.coreswiftcrm.com, which makes inbox.yourbusiness.com open your mail. Every row carries a copy button, one sentence saying what it does, and a live Active / Waiting / Needs attention badge. Press Check again after adding them.
  3. Create your inbox. The button stays disabled until receiving and sending both verify, because an address that cannot receive is worse than no address at all. Nothing is charged and no mailbox exists until then.

Inboxes follow your plan. Free 1, Starter 3, Professional 10, Enterprise 50, Agency 50 — and the setup page states your own number, read from the plan itself, so it can never promise more or fewer than the app will actually create. When you are already at that number, Create new inbox answers with the plan that unlocks more and what it costs, never a dead button.

One difference on Cloudflare: the mail records must be DNS only (grey cloud) — the orange proxy breaks mail. The inbox CNAME is the one exception: it must be Proxied (orange cloud), because Cloudflare then serves the padlock on your own address.

Logging in at the mail client takes you to a mailbox chooser. The screen is headed Choose a mailbox and lists one card per address you created, each card carrying the address and how many messages it holds; pick one and it opens. Signing in to the CRM lands on the Dashboard instead — open the Shared Inbox tab, which lists the same inboxes, each row with a LOG IN button on the right that opens your mail in a new window — at inbox.yourbusiness.com once that record is live, or at inbox.coreswiftcrm.com until it is — plus a Create new inbox button.

Files travel with your mail, both ways. When someone sends you an invoice or a signed agreement, the file is kept with the message: open it and press the file’s chip to open it in place (a picture or a PDF draws on screen, with a Download button beside it), or press the ↓ arrow to save a copy. A picture inside a message — including the logo in your own signature — is drawn where the sender put it, so a branded message shows its own logo instead of a missing image. To send a file, press Compose, pick it under Attach files (up to 10 MB each, 18 MB in total, 20 files at most) and send: the confirmation names the file the provider took, and the file is on the sent message too. A file over that limit is refused with a sentence naming the number, never dropped quietly. A sent or received file counts toward your storage the same way; at 100% only NEW attachments and files are paused. A file that arrives when the mailbox is already full is not saved — the message itself still arrives, and the reader shows a note beside the file name saying it was not saved and why. Nothing is ever deleted to make room, and no message is ever refused.

Your 30 free days start at your first verified inbox, not at signup: the days you spend waiting on DNS cost you nothing. During the trial, sending has a fair-use ceiling of 200 messages a day to protect the address’s deliverability.

When the 30 days end the address keeps working and keeps receiving. Nothing is deleted. What stops is sending — the mailbox becomes receive-only and the screen offers two buttons: Keep inbox — $1/mo (the mailbox alone) and Upgrade CRM (the whole platform). Upgrade CRM restores sending immediately. The $1 mailbox line is not on sale yet: pressing it opens its plan with a note that sales are not open and reaches our team, and nothing is charged. Every message you already have stays where it is.

What “receive-only” means in plain words: mail keeps arriving and you keep every message you already have. Only Compose and Reply stop, and both answer with the two buttons above rather than an error. Nothing is deleted at expiry, by pressing the wrong button, or by waiting — there is no deadline after which mail disappears.

One bill, never two. The $1 mailbox line is a standalone door, not an add-on. It is not on sale yet — it is absent from Plans & Billing, and pressing its card answers that sales are not open, without charging anything — so no workspace pays it today. If it does go on sale: moving up to a CRM plan ends the $1 subscription in the same step, so you pay the new plan’s price only — never $1 on top of it — and the new period starts the day you move, so nothing is prorated. Plans & Billing opens with Your bill, which states the one amount you pay, that there is one subscription, and — if your plan changed — the line that ended and the day it ended.

When your plan already includes every inbox you have, Create new inbox answers with the plan that unlocks more and what it costs, never a dead button. The $1 single-inbox line is not on sale yet — its card reaches our team and charges nothing — so move up the CRM plans for more addresses.

Sending and receiving are two separate choices

You do not have to move your mail to one company. The inbox setup page asks two questions that stand on their own: how should your mail leave (sending) and how should your mail arrive (receiving). Every option in those two lists really works — there is no placeholder in either one.

  • Sending can go through your own Mailgun account, or through any SMTP service — Amazon SES, SendGrid, Postmark, Google Workspace, or a mail server you run yourself. For those you type the server name, port, username and password that the service gave you; that is the whole setup.
  • Receiving can come through Mailgun or SendGrid, or nowhere at all if the domain only sends.

Mixing them is normal. Sending through Amazon SES while receiving through Mailgun — or sending through Mailgun and receiving through SendGrid — is a supported setup, and it is the one that lets you keep whatever already sends your mail. Changing one never changes the other.

Automated mail leaves from its own address, never your main one. Auto-responders, email sequences and automation-rule emails are sent from mg.yourbusiness.com — a subdomain CoreSwift derives automatically from the domain you added, so there is nothing extra to set up. That keeps a complaint about an automated blast away from the address your real conversations arrive on. Replies still reach you: every automated message is sent with your own inbox as the reply address, so a customer who answers lands back in your inbox.

Receiving through SendGrid

  1. Add the domain on the setup page and choose Receiving: SendGrid. No SendGrid key is needed: SendGrid posts each message to an address we give you.
  2. At your DNS host add one MX record pointing your domain at mx.sendgrid.net with priority 10. If your domain is on Cloudflare, leave it DNS only (grey cloud).
  3. Copy the SendGrid Inbound Parse address the setup page shows you — ours, ending in a long private code — then in SendGrid open Settings → Inbound Parse, add your domain and paste it as the destination.
  4. Press Check again. The receiving group turns Active once your domain’s mail really points at SendGrid, and that is when the inbox can be created.

That address is a password. SendGrid’s Inbound Parse signs nothing, so the address itself is the only thing proving a delivery is genuinely yours: anyone holding it can post mail into your inbox. Keep it out of tickets and screenshots, and never put it on a page.

Your sending records stay yours. When you receive through SendGrid, the SPF and DKIM records remain exactly as your sending service gave them — Amazon SES mints its own, SendGrid mints its own, Mailgun mints its own. The setup page says where they come from instead of inventing them. If the same domain also sends through Mailgun, Mailgun’s own sending records are shown, because they are yours and we can read them.

Switching later

Receiving can be changed without touching sending. The setup page shows Change how this domain receives under the domain; pick Mailgun, SendGrid or Nowhere. Nothing already received is deleted and your sending keys are untouched. Switching to SendGrid issues that domain’s Inbound Parse address if it did not have one.

Your own Mailgun account: the two keys, and where to copy each one

Two keys, two different jobs, and neither is a password you use to sign in here. You paste both once, on the inbox setup page; the app stores them sealed (encrypted) and uses them only for your own mail. Both are copied from your own Mailgun account.

  • The API key is the sending key. It is what lets CoreSwift hand your outgoing mail to your Mailgun account, and ask Mailgun what became of it — delivered, bounced, marked as spam. Where to copy it: sign in at Mailgun, open Settings → API Keys, and copy the Private API key (it begins with key-). One key belongs to the whole Mailgun account, so a second domain on the same account can reuse it.
  • The webhook signing key is the receiving key. Mailgun signs every message it forwards to you with this key, and CoreSwift checks that signature before it accepts the message, so nobody can post fake mail into your inbox. Without it a domain can only send: mail addressed to it is refused, which is why the setup step asks for it before it will create an inbox. Where to copy it: Mailgun, Send → Domains → your domain → Webhook signing key — a different value for each domain, unlike the API key.

A wrong or rotated key is visible, not silent. Paste a new value at any time on the same screen; the old one stops being used the moment it is saved. If Mailgun stops accepting a key, that domain’s badge reads Needs attention with a sentence saying what Mailgun answered, and the rest of the app keeps working.

Catch-all: one address that catches everything

What it is. Normally mail is accepted only for an address you created, such as info@yourbusiness.com; a message to nobody@yourbusiness.com is refused at the door. Catch-all accepts every message sent to your domain, even when that address does not exist, and files it in your inbox under Everything else (labelled MISC).

When to use it. Turn it on when people write to addresses you never created on purpose — sales@, billing@, a mistyped inof@, the address printed on an old business card — and you would rather read that mail than have it bounce. Leave it off when you want silence: with catch-all off, mail to an unknown address is refused, which is also how a sender learns they have the wrong address.

How to switch it. Sidebar → Inbox → Email Domains & Sending, find the domain and use its Catch-all column: Enable catch-all turns it on, Disable turns it off. It is set per domain, so two domains on one account can differ.

It follows the plan, not the domain. On this plan ladder catch-all is included from Professional ($79) upward — Professional, Enterprise and Agency — and is not sold on Free or Starter. Pressing the button on a plan that does not include it answers with the plan that unlocks it and what it costs, and nothing changes. If the workspace later moves back to a plan without it, your setting is remembered but mail to an unknown address is refused again; the column then reads off — not on your plan, with a Clear button that forgets the setting.

What catch-all is not. It does not create addresses. A catch-all message is not a mailbox, cannot be signed into, and does not start a trial — it is one bucket inside the inbox you already have.

Your logo and signature on outgoing mail

One logo and one workspace signature, both applied automatically. Open Email Branding in the workspace menu: upload the company logo, then fill in the signature — name, title, company, phone and one short line — and press Save signature. From then on every new message and every reply opens with the logo and the signature already in place under the text you type, so you type only the subject and the message.

You never size the logo. Any PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP up to 2 MB is accepted and the app resizes it for email, so it looks sharp and the right size in Gmail, Outlook and Apple Mail on a phone as well as on a desktop. The preview on the Email Branding screen shows what your customers will see.

Per address, if you want. In the mail client press Signature and choose, for one address: use the workspace signature, write its own wording (a blank field falls back to the workspace value for that field), or send without a signature — useful for a no-reply address. Every other address keeps following the workspace signature.

Leave it off one message. The compose window shows the signature under the message with an Include the signature on this message box already ticked. Untick it and that one message — or that one reply — goes without the signature. Nothing is saved, so the next message starts signed again.

Changing or deleting the logo never breaks mail you already sent. The logo travels inside each message rather than being fetched from a link, so a message sent last month still shows the logo it was sent with even after the logo is replaced or removed. New messages use the new logo immediately.

Storage: the meter, the three warnings, and what a full mailbox does

Every account has a storage allowance, and it grows with the plan. Free starts at 2 GB; Starter 10 GB, Professional 50 GB, Enterprise 100 GB and Agency 250 GB. (Pro and the $1 mailbox line carry 10 GB and 5 GB in the catalogue but are not on sale, so they are not part of this ladder.) Storage counts the mail you have received and sent, plus the logo and profile pictures you upload.

The meter is on the mail screen all the time, at the bottom of the left-hand column, and it states the real figures — for example “1.5 of 2 GB used (75%)” — never just “storage”.

You are warned three times, and at most once per level per month. At 50%, 75% and 99% full a banner appears above the message list and stays there until space is freed. The account owner also gets one email per level per month, carrying the same numbers, what to do, and a link to the mail screen and the plans. The same level is never emailed twice in one month.

When the reminders arrive. The figures are checked whenever you open your mail, and again in the background every ten minutes, so a mailbox nobody opens is still warned by email. One email per level per month is the most you will ever receive — the same level cannot be mailed twice in one month, however much mail arrives in between.

A full mailbox never loses mail. At 100% plain mail still arrives and is stored, sending still works, and nothing is deleted to make room. The only thing that pauses is adding a new attachment or file (for example uploading a logo): that answers with a sentence naming the figures and the two ways out — delete or archive mail (Trash and Spam count toward the figure until emptied), or upgrade for more storage. Everything works again the moment the figure drops below the allowance.

Time-based retention is only a far backstop. Mail is not removed on a clock: the default backstop is ten years (3,650 days), and the mail screen always states the window that applies to the account, together with the date the oldest message would reach it. Freeing space is always your choice — by deleting or archiving — never an automatic purge to make room.

Seeing a domain's DNS records, and removing a domain you no longer want

Every domain on Email Domains & Sending has two buttons beside its verification badge.

  • DNS records asks your own mail provider for the exact values that domain needs — the receiving MX records, the SPF text record and the DKIM key — and shows them in a table with a Copy button on every value. Add each one at your DNS host (Cloudflare: DNS → Records), then press Re-check. Mail records stay DNS only (grey cloud); only the branded inbox CNAME is proxied. This works on every domain at any time, including the second and third domain you add to an existing account.
  • Remove this domain (inside the DNS records window) deletes a domain you no longer want — and the inboxes that live on it. Mail already received stays in your Inbox; only the addresses go away. Because that cannot be undone, the app asks you to type the domain name to confirm, and refuses every other answer.
